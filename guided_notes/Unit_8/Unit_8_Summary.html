8.1Area of Polygons and Composite Figures

  • Parallelogram: A = bh. Triangle: A = 12bh. Trapezoid: A = 12(b1 + b2)h. The height is perpendicular to the base.
  • Regular polygon: A = 12aP. The apothem a goes from the center to the middle of a side. P is the perimeter. Use a 30-60-90 triangle to find a for a hexagon.
  • A composite figure is made of simple shapes. Add the parts, or subtract a missing part from a bigger shape.
  • To find a missing length, put the known numbers in the formula and solve.
Example: a regular hexagon has sides of 6 in. Then a = 3√3 and A = 12(3√3)(36) = 54√3 ≈ 93.5 in2.

8.2Cross Sections and Solids of Revolution

  • A polyhedron has flat faces, edges and vertices. Euler's formula: F + V − E = 2.
  • A prism has two matching bases. A pyramid has one base and an apex. A cylinder and a cone have circular bases. A sphere is all the points the same distance from the center.
  • A cross section is the shape a plane makes when it cuts a solid. Parallel to the base of a cylinder or cone: a circle. Through the axis of a cylinder: a rectangle. Through the apex of a cone: a triangle. Every cut of a sphere: a circle.
  • A solid of revolution is made by spinning a flat shape about an axis. A rectangle makes a cylinder. A right triangle makes a cone.
Example: a solid has 6 faces and 8 vertices. 6 + 8 − E = 2, so E = 12.

8.3Surface Area and Nets

  • A net is a solid unfolded flat. Surface area is the area of the net.
  • Prism: SA = 2B + Ph. The lateral area L = Ph is the sides only.
  • Cylinder: SA = 2 πr2 + 2 πrh.
  • Pyramid: SA = B + 12Pℓ. Cone: SA = πr2 + πrℓ. The slant height ℓ is along a face, not the inside height. Find h or ℓ with the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Sphere: SA = 4 πr2.
Example: a cylinder with r = 3 and h = 10 has SA = 2 π(9) + 2 π(3)(10) = 78 π ≈ 245.0.

8.4Volume of Prisms and Cylinders

  • Volume is the number of cubic units that fill a solid.
  • Prism: V = Bh. Cylinder: V = πr2h. B is the area of the base.
  • Cavalieri's principle: solids with the same height and the same cross-section area at every level have the same volume. A slanted (oblique) prism or cylinder uses the vertical height.
  • Change units with a ratio: 1 yd3 = 27 ft3. 1 ft3 ≈ 7.48 gallons.
Example: a slab is 30 ft by 12 ft by 13 ft. V = 120 ft3, and 120 ÷ 27 ≈ 4.4 yd3.

8.5Volume of Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

  • Pyramid: V = 13Bh. Cone: V = 13πr2h. Each is one third of the prism or cylinder with the same base and height.
  • Sphere: V = 43πr3. A hemisphere is half: V = 23πr3.
  • Use the height, not the slant height. If you are given the slant height, find h first.
  • A frustum is a cone or pyramid with the top cut off. Subtract the small solid from the large one.
  • For a composite solid, add or subtract the volumes of the parts.
Example: a cone with r = 6 and ℓ = 10 has h = 8 and V = 13π(36)(8) = 96 π ≈ 301.6.

8.6Density and Modeling

  • Density = massvolume. Weight = density · volume. Water: 62.4 lb per ft3, or 1 g per cm3. Concrete: about 150 lb per ft3. An object floats if its density is less than water.
  • Population density = peoplesquare miles.
  • If lengths are multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k2 and volumes by k3.
  • For a plan: draw it, make the units match, find the volume, then find the cost, weight, bags or trips. Round up for whole items.
Example: a slab is 10 ft by 15 ft by 13 ft. V = 50 ft3 and the weight is 150 · 50 = 7500 lb.
