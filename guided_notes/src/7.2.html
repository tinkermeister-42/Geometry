7.2.1Area of a Circle

  • Cut a circle into thin wedges. Slide them together. The shape is close to a {{a}}parallelogram{{/a}}.

  • Area = base · height, so A = {{a}}πr2{{/a}}.
  • Use the {{a}}radius{{/a}}, not the diameter. Area is in {{a}}square{{/a}} units.

7.2.3Shaded Regions and Composite Shapes

To find the area of a shaded region, {{a}}add{{/a}} or {{a}}subtract{{/a}} the areas of simple shapes.
EX 12. A ring (an annulus) has an outer radius of 10 feet and an inner radius of 6 feet. Find its area.
{{fig 7.2_ring|a ring with outer radius 10 and inner radius 6|2.0in}}

Area = outer circle − inner circle

{{a}}

A = π(10)2 − π(6)2 = 100 π − 36 π = 64 π ≈ 201.06 ft2

{{/a}}
Do not subtract the radii first
The ring is π(10)2 − π(6)2. It is not π(10 − 6)2. {{a}}Square{{/a}} each radius first, then {{a}}subtract{{/a}}.
