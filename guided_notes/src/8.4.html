8.4.1Volume of a Prism

  • Volume is the amount of {{a}}space{{/a}} inside a solid. We count {{a}}cubic units{{/a}}.
  • Each layer of the box below has 4 · 3 = {{a}}12{{/a}} cubes. There are {{a}}2{{/a}} layers. Total: {{a}}24{{/a}} cubes.
  • For any prism, V = {{a}}Bh{{/a}}. B is the area of the {{a}}base{{/a}}.

8.4.3Slanted Solids

  • Cavalieri's principle: two solids with the same {{a}}height{{/a}} and the same cross-section {{a}}area{{/a}} at every level have the same {{a}}volume{{/a}}.
  • A leaning solid is {{a}}oblique{{/a}}. Use the {{a}}straight-up height{{/a}}, not the slanted side.
{{fig 8.4_coins|a straight stack of coins and a slanted stack of the same coins with the same height|4.6in}}

{{fig 8.4_oblique|an oblique cylinder with radius 4 feet, slanted side 13 feet and straight-up height 12 feet|1.9in}}

The 13 ft side {{a}}is not used{{/a}}.

V = π(4)2(12) = {{a}}192 π{{/a}}

≈ {{a}}603.2 cubic feet{{/a}}

8.4.4Changing Units

  • 1 yd3 = {{a}}27{{/a}} ft3 because 3 · 3 · 3 = 27.
  • 1 ft3 = {{a}}1728{{/a}} in3 because 12 · 12 · 12.
  • 1 ft3 ≈ {{a}}7.48{{/a}} gallons.
  • Make all lengths the {{a}}same unit{{/a}} before you multiply. 4 in = {{a}}{{f 1/3}}{{/a}} ft.

EX 18. A box has length x + 3, width x, and height 4. The volume is 160 cubic units. Find x.
{{a}}

4x(x + 3) = 160, so x2 + 3x − 40 = 0

(x + 8)(x − 5) = 0

{{/a}}

x = {{a}}5{{/a}} (a length cannot be {{a}}negative{{/a}}). The box is {{a}}8 by 5 by 4{{/a}}.

EX 19. A box has length x + 2, width x, and height 3. The volume is 105 cubic units. Find x.your turn
