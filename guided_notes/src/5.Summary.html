5.1Ratios and Proportions

  • A ratio compares two numbers by division: 2 : 3, {{f 2/3}}, or "2 to 3". Use the same units.
  • A proportion says that two ratios are equal. Cross products: {{f a/b}} = {{f c/d}} means ad = bc.
  • Put the same kind of number in the same place in both ratios.
  • A scale like 1  in : 20  ft turns a drawing length into a real length, and the other way.
  • To split a total in the ratio 3 : 5, write the parts 3x and 5x and add them to the total.
{{fig 5.1_ang|triangle with angles 2x, 3x and 4x|2.3in}}
Example: {{f x/12}} = {{f 5/8}} gives 8x = 60, so x = 7.5. Angles in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4: 9x = 180, so x = 20 and the angles are 40°, 60°, 80°.

5.2Similar Figures and Scale Factor

  • Similar figures (∼) have the same shape. Matching angles are equal and matching sides are proportional.
  • The order of the letters in △ ABC ∼ △ DEF shows which parts match.
  • The scale factor k is the new length divided by the old length. The other direction uses the reciprocal.
  • To find a missing side, write a proportion with matching sides, then use cross products.
  • Perimeters change by k. Areas change by k2.
  • Equal angles alone are not enough. Two rectangles with equal angles can be different shapes.
{{fig 5.2_sim|two similar triangles with sides 4, 6, 8 and 6, 9, 12|3.6in}}
Example: If k = 1.5, a side of 4 becomes 6. A perimeter of 18 becomes 27. An area of 10 becomes 1.52 · 10 = 22.5.

5.3Similar Triangles

  • AA: two pairs of equal angles make triangles similar.
  • SSS: all three pairs of sides are proportional.
  • SAS: two pairs of sides are proportional and the included angles are equal.
  • If {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}, then △ ADE ∼ △ ABC. Draw the triangles apart and use the whole side AB for the big triangle.
  • On a grid, use the distance formula for side ratios and slopes for parallel sides.
{{fig 5.3_aa|two triangles with two matching angle marks|3.3in}}
Example: Sides 4, 6, 8 and 10, 15, 20 have equal ratios of 2.5. The triangles are similar by SSS.

5.4Triangle Proportionality

  • If {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}, then {{f AD/DB}} = {{f AE/EC}}.
  • Converse: if the two ratios are equal, then {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}.
  • A midsegment joins the midpoints of two sides. It is parallel to the third side and half as long.
  • Three parallel lines cut two transversals into proportional parts.
  • On a grid, use the midpoint formula and slopes to check a midsegment.
{{fig 5.4_midseg|triangle ABC with midsegment DE|2.1in}}
Example: AD = 6, DB = 4, and AE = 9. Then {{f 6/4}} = {{f 9/x}}, so x = 6.

5.5Indirect Measurement

  • Use similar triangles to find a length you cannot measure.
  • Four steps: draw a sketch, name the similar triangles, write a proportion, solve and use units.
  • Shadows: a person and a tree at the same time make similar right triangles.
  • Mirrors: the angle in equals the angle out. Use eye height.
  • River: with stakes B, C, D and point E, {{f AB/DE}} = {{f BC/CD}}.
{{fig 5.5_shadow|a person and a tree with their shadows|3.8in}}
Example: A 5 ft person has an 8 ft shadow. A tree has a 40 ft shadow. {{f 5/8}} = {{f h/40}}, so h = 25 ft.
