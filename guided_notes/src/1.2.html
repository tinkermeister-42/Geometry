1.2.1Length on a Number Line

  • A ruler is a {{a}}number line{{/a}}. To find a length, {{a}}subtract{{/a}} the two numbers at the ends.
  • For points at a and b: AB = {{a}}|a − b|{{/a}}
  • A length is never {{a}}negative{{/a}}, so we use absolute value.

1.2.3Congruent Segments and Midpoints

  • Segments with the same length are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}. The symbol is {{a}}≅{{/a}}. {{seg AB}} ≅ {{seg CD}} means {{a}}AB{{/a}} = {{a}}CD{{/a}}.
  • Use ≅ for {{a}}figures{{/a}}. Use = for {{a}}numbers{{/a}}.
  • The midpoint is the point exactly {{a}}halfway{{/a}} between the endpoints. It makes two {{a}}congruent{{/a}} segments.
  • A segment bisector is any point, line, ray, or segment that goes through the {{a}}midpoint{{/a}}.

Summary
additionbetweenbisectorcongruentmidpointsubtract

1. To find a length on a number line, {{a}}subtract{{/a}} and use absolute value.

2. AB + BC = AC only when B is {{a}}between{{/a}} A and C.

3. A {{a}}midpoint{{/a}} makes two {{a}}congruent{{/a}} segments.

4. An angle {{a}}bisector{{/a}} makes two congruent angles.

5. Segments and angles both use {{a}}addition{{/a}}: the parts add to the whole.

Find the Mistake

{{a}}

He treated B like a midpoint. B is only between the ends, so the parts add to the whole: (4x − 2) + (2x + 8) = 42, 6x + 6 = 42, x = 6.
