3.4.1Scale Factor

  • A dilation changes the size of a figure from a fixed point, the {{a}}center{{/a}}. The shape stays the same.
  • If k > 1, the image is larger: an {{a}}enlargement{{/a}}. If 0 < k < 1, the image is smaller: a {{a}}reduction{{/a}}.
  • With the center at the origin: (x, y) → ({{a}}kx{{/a}}, {{a}}ky{{/a}}).
EX 1. Dilate points

The center is the origin. Dilate P(4, −6) with k = {{f 1/2}}. Dilate Q(−2, 5) with k = 3.

P′ = ({{f 1/2}} · 4, {{f 1/2}} · (−6)) = {{a}}(2, −3){{/a}}

Q′ = (3 · (−2), 3 · 5) = {{a}}(−6, 15){{/a}}

P′ is closer to the origin, so this is a {{a}}reduction{{/a}}. Q′ is farther, so this is an {{a}}enlargement{{/a}}.

3.4.2What Changes and What Stays

  • Every length is multiplied by {{a}}k{{/a}}.
  • Every angle measure stays the {{a}}same{{/a}}.
  • Each side is {{a}}parallel{{/a}} to its image. (They have the same slope.)
  • A dilation is {{a}}not{{/a}} a rigid motion, because lengths change (unless k = 1).
EX 5. Check a dilation
{{fig 3.4_tri|triangle ABC and its image with k = 2|3.0in}}

In the triangle with A(2, 1), B(4, 1), C(2, 3) and k = 2, compare the sides.

AB = {{a}}2{{/a}} and A′B′ = {{a}}4{{/a}}. A′B′ = 2 · AB.

BC = 4 + 4 = {{a}}22{{/a}} and B′C′ = 16 + 16 = {{a}}42{{/a}}. B′C′ = 2 · BC.

Slope of {{seg BC}}: {{f 3 − 1/2 − 4}} = {{a}}−1{{/a}}. Slope of {{seg B'C'}}: {{f 6 − 2/4 − 8}} = {{a}}−1{{/a}}.

The slopes are equal, so {{seg BC}} ∥ {{seg B'C'}}. The angle at A is 90° and the angle at A′ is {{a}}90°{{/a}}.

Same shape, not same size
A dilation makes a similar figure. It does not make a congruent one. Do not say that a dilation keeps lengths. It multiplies them by k.

3.4.3Finding the Scale Factor

  • k = {{f image length/original length}}
  • If k is more than 1, it is an {{a}}enlargement{{/a}}. If k is less than 1, it is a {{a}}reduction{{/a}}.
EX 7. Find k from a figure
{{fig 3.4_find|rectangle WXYZ and its larger image|3.1in}}

Rectangle WXYZ is dilated about the origin to W′X′Y′Z′. Find the scale factor.

Original width WZ = 4. Image width W′Z′ = {{a}}6{{/a}}.

k = {{f 6/4}} = {{a}}{{f 3/2}}{{/a}}

Check with a point: W(2, 2) → W′(3, 3), and 3 ÷ 2 = {{a}}{{f 3/2}}{{/a}}.

k > 1, so it is an {{a}}enlargement{{/a}}.

Maps and models
A model car has a scale of 1:24. That is k = {{f 1/24}}. A real car 180 inches long becomes a model 180 · {{f 1/24}} = 7.5 inches long.

3.4.4A Center That Is Not the Origin

  • Step 1: Subtract the center: (x − h, y − j).
  • Step 2: Multiply by k.
  • Step 3: {{a}}Add{{/a}} the center back.
EX 9. Dilate about a point
{{fig 3.4_center|triangle ABC and the center P|3.1in}}

Dilate △ ABC with k = 2 about the center P(1, 1). A(3, 1), B(3, 3), C(2, 3).

A: (3 − 1, 1 − 1) = (2, 0). Times 2: (4, 0). Add back: A′ = {{a}}(5, 1){{/a}}.

B: (2, 2) → (4, 4) → B′ = {{a}}(5, 5){{/a}}.

C: (1, 2) → (2, 4) → C′ = {{a}}(3, 5){{/a}}.

Summary
anglescenterenlargementmultipliedparallelreduction

1. A dilation uses a {{a}}center{{/a}} and a scale factor k. The shape stays the same.

2. If k > 1 it is an {{a}}enlargement{{/a}}. If 0 < k < 1 it is a {{a}}reduction{{/a}}.

3. Lengths are {{a}}multiplied{{/a}} by k. {{a}}angles{{/a}} stay the same.

4. A side and its image are {{a}}parallel{{/a}}.
