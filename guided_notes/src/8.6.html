8.6.1Density and Weight

  • Density = {{a}}{{f mass/volume}}{{/a}}. Weight = density × {{a}}volume{{/a}}.
  • Water: {{a}}62.4{{/a}} lb per ft3, or {{a}}1{{/a}} g per cm3. Concrete: about {{a}}150{{/a}} lb per ft3.
  • An object floats if its density is {{a}}less than{{/a}} water. It sinks if its density is {{a}}greater than{{/a}} water.

V = 12 · 20 · {{f 1/3}} = {{a}}80 ft3{{/a}}

8.6.2Population Density

Population density = {{a}}{{f people/square miles}}{{/a}}.
EX 8. City A has 120,000 people in 40 square miles. City B has 75,000 people in 15 square miles. Which is more crowded?

B: {{a}}75,000 ÷ 15 = 5000 people per square mile{{/a}}

8.6.3Scaling Lengths, Areas, and Volumes

If lengths are multiplied by k:
  • Lengths: × {{a}}k{{/a}}
  • Areas: × {{a}}k2{{/a}}
  • Volumes, and weights of the same material: × {{a}}k3{{/a}}

Summary
densityk2k3sinksupvolume

1. Weight = {{a}}density{{/a}} × volume. An object that floats has density less than water.

2. If lengths scale by k, areas scale by {{a}}k2{{/a}} and volumes by {{a}}k3{{/a}}.

3. For jobs that need whole items (bags, trips), round {{a}}up{{/a}}.
