1.1.1Points, Lines, and Planes

  • A point is an exact {{a}}location{{/a}}. It has no {{a}}size{{/a}}. We name it with a {{a}}capital letter{{/a}}.
  • A line is straight and goes on forever in {{a}}both directions{{/a}}. The arrows show that it never stops.
  • A plane is a {{a}}flat{{/a}} surface that goes on forever in every direction.
  • Through any two points there is exactly {{a}}one{{/a}} line.
  • Points on the same line are {{a}}collinear{{/a}}. Points in the same plane are {{a}}coplanar{{/a}}.

1.1.2Segments and Rays

  • A segment is part of a line between two {{a}}endpoints{{/a}}.
  • A ray starts at one endpoint and goes on forever in {{a}}one direction{{/a}}.
ObjectPictureNameSymbol
segment{{fig 1.1_segment|a segment with endpoints A and B|1.3in}}segment AB{{a}}{{seg AB}}{{/a}}
ray{{fig 1.1_ray|a ray that starts at A and goes through B|1.3in}}ray AB{{a}}{{ray AB}}{{/a}}
Order matters for a ray
{{seg AB}} and {{seg BA}} are {{a}}the same{{/a}} segment. {{ray AB}} and {{ray BA}} are {{a}}different{{/a}} rays. The first letter of a ray is always its {{a}}endpoint{{/a}}.
Segment or length?
{{seg AB}}, with a bar, is the {{a}}segment{{/a}}. AB, with no bar, is its {{a}}length{{/a}}, a number like AB = 5.

1.1.3Angles

  • An angle is two {{a}}rays{{/a}} with the same endpoint.
  • The rays are the {{a}}sides{{/a}}. The shared endpoint is the {{a}}vertex{{/a}}.
  • We measure angles in {{a}}degrees{{/a}} (°). One full turn is {{a}}360°{{/a}}.
{{fig 1.1_angle_parts|angle ABC with vertex B, labelled 1 inside, its two sides marked|2.9in}}

Three ways to name this angle:

  Three letters, vertex in the middle: {{a}}∠ABC or ∠CBA{{/a}}   The vertex only: {{a}}∠B{{/a}}   The number: {{a}}∠1{{/a}}

This angle measures 130°. Write it with symbols: {{a}}{{mang ABC}} = 130°{{/a}}

EX 7. What angle do the hands of a clock make at 2:00?
{{fig 1.1_clock|a clock showing 2 o'clock, with the angle between the hands marked|1.6in}}

A full turn is 360°. A clock has 12 hour marks.

One hour mark to the next: 360° ÷ 12 = {{a}}30°{{/a}}

At 2:00 the hands are 2 hour marks apart: 2 × 30° = {{a}}60°{{/a}}

The angle is {{a}}acute{{/a}} because {{a}}60° < 90°{{/a}}

EX 8. What angle do the hands make at 5:00? Classify the angle.your turn
{{a}}

5 × 30° = 150°. It is between 90° and 180°, so the angle is obtuse.

1.1.5What a Figure Shows

A drawing can fool your eyes. We only trust what we are {{a}}told{{/a}} and what the figure {{a}}marks{{/a}}.
  • A small square at a vertex means a {{a}}right angle{{/a}}.
  • Matching {{a}}tick marks{{/a}} mean segments have the same length.

1. A {{a}}segment{{/a}} has two endpoints. A {{a}}ray{{/a}} has one.

2. The first letter in the name of a ray is its {{a}}endpoint{{/a}}.

3. In the name ∠ABC, the {{a}}vertex{{/a}} is B.

4. An angle of 120° is {{a}}obtuse{{/a}}.

5. We trust what a figure {{a}}marks{{/a}}, not what it looks like.
