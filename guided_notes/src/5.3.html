5.3.1AA Similarity

  • The three angles of a triangle add to {{a}}180°{{/a}}.
  • If two angles of a triangle equal two angles of another triangle, the third angles are {{a}}equal{{/a}} too.
  • AA similarity: two pairs of equal angles make the triangles {{a}}similar{{/a}}.

b. Are the triangles similar? Write the statement.

{{a}}

a. m∠C = 180 − 50 − 60 = 70°. m∠F = 180 − 50 − 60 = 70°.

b. Two pairs of angles are equal (and the third pair is too). The triangles are similar by AA.

5.3.2SSS and SAS Similarity

  • SSS similarity: all three pairs of sides are {{a}}proportional{{/a}}.
  • SAS similarity: two pairs of sides are proportional and the {{a}}included{{/a}} angles are equal.
  • The included angle is the angle {{a}}between{{/a}} the two sides.
EX 3. SSS. Triangle 1 has sides 4, 6, and 8. Triangle 2 has sides 10, 15, and 20. Are the triangles similar? Then try 6, 8, 10 and 9, 12, 16.
{{a}}

Match the shortest with the shortest, and so on.

{{f 10/4}} = 2.5, {{f 15/6}} = 2.5, {{f 20/8}} = 2.5

All three ratios are equal. The triangles are similar by SSS.

For 6, 8, 10 and 9, 12, 16: {{f 9/6}} = 1.5, {{f 12/8}} = 1.5, but {{f 16/10}} = 1.6. The ratios are not all equal. Not similar.

{{/a}}

5.3.3Which Test Works?

  • Two angles known: use {{a}}AA{{/a}}.
  • Three sides known: use {{a}}SSS{{/a}}.
  • Two sides and the angle between them: use {{a}}SAS{{/a}}.
  • If the test fails, the triangles are {{a}}not{{/a}} similar, or you cannot tell.

5.3.4Overlapping Triangles

  • If {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}, then {{ang ADE}} = {{ang ABC}} because they are {{a}}corresponding{{/a}} angles.
  • The angle at A is {{a}}shared{{/a}} by both triangles.
  • So △ ADE ∼ △ ABC by {{a}}AA{{/a}}.
  • To solve, draw the triangles {{a}}separately{{/a}} and match the sides.
EX 9. Nested triangles. {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}. AD = 6, DB = 3, and DE = 8. Find BC.
{{fig 5.3_nested|triangle ABC with a right angle at B; segment DE parallel to BC with AD equal to 6, DB equal to 3 and DE equal to 8; the same two triangles drawn apart, ADE and ABC with BC equal to x|5.2in}}
{{a}}

∠A is shared. {{ang ADE}} = {{ang ABC}} (corresponding angles). So △ ADE ∼ △ ABC by AA.

The big triangle uses the whole side: AB = 6 + 3 = 9.

{{f AD/AB}} = {{f DE/BC}}, so {{f 6/9}} = {{f 8/x}}.

6x = 72, so x = 12.

{{/a}}

{{/a}}

5.3.5Similar Triangles on a Grid

  • Use the distance formula to find the {{a}}side lengths{{/a}} and then check the ratios.
  • Matching sides that are parallel have equal {{a}}slopes{{/a}}, so the angles are equal.
  • If all three pairs of sides are parallel, use {{a}}AA{{/a}}.
EX 13. Check with distances. Show that △ ABC ∼ △ DEF. Find k.
{{fig 5.3_grid|triangle ABC with vertices A(1,1), B(3,1), C(1,4) and triangle DEF with vertices D(5,1), E(9,1), F(5,7)|2.9in}}
{{a}}

A(1, 1), B(3, 1), C(1, 4). D(5, 1), E(9, 1), F(5, 7).

AB = 2, AC = 3, BC = √22 + 32 = √13.

DE = 4, DF = 6, EF = √42 + 62 = √52 = 2√13.

{{f DE/AB}} = 2, {{f DF/AC}} = 2, {{f EF/BC}} = {{f 2√13/√13}} = 2.

All ratios are 2. Similar by SSS, with k = 2.

{{/a}}

Summary
AAcorrespondingdistanceSASSSS

1. If two angles of one triangle equal two angles of another, the triangles are similar by {{a}}AA{{/a}}.

2. For {{a}}SSS{{/a}} similarity, all three pairs of sides are proportional.

3. For {{a}}SAS{{/a}} similarity, two pairs of sides are proportional and the included angles are equal.

4. If {{seg DE}} ∥ {{seg BC}}, then △ ADE ∼ △ ABC because they share ∠A and have {{a}}corresponding{{/a}} angles.

5. On a grid, use the {{a}}distance{{/a}} formula to check side ratios and slopes to check for parallel sides.
