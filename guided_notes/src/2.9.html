2.9.1Is It a Right Triangle?

  • Way 1: two sides have slopes with a product of {{a}}−1{{/a}}.
  • Way 2: the squares of the sides satisfy {{a}}a2 + b2 = c2{{/a}}.
  • Always end with: "Because ..., the triangle is a ...".
EX 1. Show that triangle ABC with A(2, 1), B(8, 3), and C(1, 4) is a right triangle. Use both ways.
{{fig 2.9_triangle|triangle ABC with A(2,1), B(8,3), C(1,4)|2.2in}}

Way 1. Slope of AB: 3 − 18 − 2 = {{a}}13{{/a}}. Slope of AC: 4 − 11 − 2 = {{a}}−3{{/a}}.

The product is 13 · (−3) = {{a}}−1{{/a}}.

Way 2. AB2 = 62 + 22 = {{a}}40{{/a}}. AC2 = (−1)2 + 32 = {{a}}10{{/a}}. BC2 = (−7)2 + 12 = {{a}}50{{/a}}.

AB2 + AC2 = 40 + 10 = 50 = BC2.

Because {{a}}the slopes of AB and AC have a product of −1 (and AB2 + AC2 = BC2), AB ⊥ AC. Triangle ABC is a right triangle with the right angle at A.{{/a}}

EX 2. Is triangle PQR with P(0, 0), Q(6, 2), and R(4, 6) a right triangle?

Slopes: PQ: 26 = 13. PR: 64 = 32. QR: 6 − 24 − 6 = −2.

Products: 13 · 32 = 12, 13 · (−2) = −23, 32 · (−2) = −3. None is −1.

Check by distance: PQ2 = 40, PR2 = 52, QR2 = 20. 40 + 20 = 60, which is not 52.

Because {{a}}no pair of sides is perpendicular, triangle PQR is not a right triangle.{{/a}}

2.9.2Isosceles or Equilateral?

  • Isosceles: at least {{a}}two{{/a}} equal sides.
  • Equilateral: {{a}}three{{/a}} equal sides.
  • Compare the lengths. If you can, compare the {{a}}squares{{/a}} of the lengths (no radicals to deal with).
EX 4. Is triangle ABC with A(1, 1), B(7, 1), and C(4, 5) isosceles? Is it equilateral?

AB = |7 − 1| = {{a}}6{{/a}}.

AC = 32 + 42 = {{a}}5{{/a}}. BC = (−3)2 + 42 = {{a}}5{{/a}}.

Because {{a}}AC = BC = 5, triangle ABC is isosceles. It is not equilateral, because AB = 6 is different.{{/a}}

EX 5. Show that the triangle with A(1, 1), B(5, 1), and C(3, 1 + 23) is equilateral.

AB = 4.

AC = 22 + (23)2 = 4 + 12 = {{a}}4{{/a}}. BC = (−2)2 + (23)2 = 16 = 4.

Because {{a}}AB = AC = BC = 4, triangle ABC is equilateral.{{/a}}

{{a}}

2.9.3Four-Sided Shapes

  • A parallelogram has {{a}}both pairs{{/a}} of opposite sides parallel.
  • A rectangle is a parallelogram with a {{a}}right angle{{/a}}.
  • A rhombus has {{a}}four{{/a}} equal sides.
  • A square is both a {{a}}rectangle{{/a}} and a {{a}}rhombus{{/a}}.
ShapeWhat to check with coordinates
Parallelogram{{a}}Opposite sides have equal slopes (both pairs){{/a}}
Rectangle{{a}}A parallelogram, and two sides at a corner have slopes with product −1{{/a}}
Rhombus{{a}}All four sides have the same length{{/a}}
Square{{a}}A rectangle and a rhombus together{{/a}}
EX 7. Is ABCD with A(1, 1), B(5, 2), C(6, 5), D(2, 4) a parallelogram? A rectangle? A rhombus?
{{fig 2.9_para|quadrilateral ABCD with A(1,1), B(5,2), C(6,5), D(2,4)|2.0in}}

Slopes: AB: 2 − 15 − 1 = 14. DC: 5 − 46 − 2 = {{a}}14{{/a}}. AD: 4 − 12 − 1 = 3. BC: 5 − 26 − 5 = {{a}}3{{/a}}.

Because {{a}}both pairs of opposite sides have equal slopes, ABCD is a parallelogram.{{/a}}

Rectangle? 14 · 3 = 34, not −1. The corner at A is not a right angle. It is not a rectangle.

Rhombus? AB = 17 and AD = 10. They are not equal. It is not a rhombus.

So ABCD is a parallelogram and nothing more specific.

EX 8. Is ABCD with A(2, 1), B(6, 3), C(5, 5), D(1, 3) a rectangle? A square?
{{fig 2.9_rect|quadrilateral ABCD with A(2,1), B(6,3), C(5,5), D(1,3)|2.0in}}

Slopes: AB and DC are both 12. AD and BC are both {{a}}−2{{/a}}. It is a parallelogram.

At A: 12 · (−2) = {{a}}−1{{/a}}. There is a right angle.

Because {{a}}ABCD is a parallelogram with a right angle, it is a rectangle.{{/a}}

Square? AB = 20 and AD = 5. Not equal. So it is a rectangle but not a square.

EX 9. Is ABCD with A(1, 1), B(6, 1), C(9, 5), D(4, 5) a rhombus? A square?

AB = 5. BC = 32 + 42 = {{a}}5{{/a}}. CD = 5. DA = (−3)2 + (−4)2 = 5.

Because {{a}}all four sides have length 5, ABCD is a rhombus.{{/a}}

Square? AB is horizontal (slope 0). AD has slope 43. They are not perpendicular. So it is a rhombus but not a square.

2.9.4Looks Like, or Is?

  • A shape that looks like a square can be a {{a}}rhombus{{/a}}.
  • A shape that looks like a rectangle can be a {{a}}parallelogram{{/a}} with a corner not quite 90°.
  • Slopes 17 and 7 are not perpendicular. Their product is 1, not {{a}}−1{{/a}}.
EX 11. The shape ABCD with A(1, 1), B(8, 2), C(9, 9), D(2, 8) looks like a tilted square. Is it a square?
{{fig 2.9_looks|rhombus ABCD with A(1,1), B(8,2), C(9,9), D(2,8)|2.0in}}

AB = 72 + 12 = 50. BC = 12 + 72 = 50. CD = 50. DA = 50. All four sides are equal, so it is a rhombus.

Slope of AB: 17. Slope of AD: 71 = 7. The product is 17 · 7 = {{a}}1{{/a}}, not −1.

Because {{a}}AB and AD are not perpendicular, ABCD is not a square. It is a rhombus.{{/a}}

EX 12. The shape ABCD with A(2, 1), B(12, 2), C(11, 11), D(1, 10) looks like a rectangle. Is it?
{{fig 2.9_nearrect|parallelogram ABCD with A(2,1), B(12,2), C(11,11), D(1,10)|2.2in}}

Slopes: AB and DC are both 110. AD and BC are both 9−1 = −9. It is a parallelogram.

At A: 110 · (−9) = {{a}}−910{{/a}}. This is close to −1, but it is not −1.

Because {{a}}the product is −910, there is no right angle. ABCD is not a rectangle. It is a parallelogram.{{/a}}

Counterexample: four equal sides

Does "four equal sides" make a square? No. The rhombus in the example has four equal sides and is not a square. One example that breaks a rule is a counterexample. It is enough to show the rule is false.

Error analysis: Hnin's mistake

Hnin says: "ABCD with A(1, 1), B(5, 2), C(6, 5), D(2, 4) is a rectangle because its opposite sides are equal."

The opposite sides are equal, but that is also true for a parallelogram that is not a rectangle. The missing check is a right angle. Here 14 · 3 = 34, so there is no right angle. It is not a rectangle.

2.9.5Finding the Fourth Vertex

  • For parallelogram ABCD: the step A → D equals the step {{a}}B → C{{/a}}.
  • D = A + C − B. The diagonals AC and BD have the same {{a}}midpoint{{/a}}.
EX 14. Three vertices of parallelogram ABCD are A(1, 1), B(6, 2), and C(8, 6). Find D. Check your answer.
{{fig 2.9_fourth|three points A(1,1), B(6,2), C(8,6) on a grid|2.4in}}

Step from B to C: (8 − 6, 6 − 2) = ({{a}}2{{/a}}, {{a}}4{{/a}}).

D = (1 + 2, 1 + 4) = ({{a}}3{{/a}}, {{a}}5{{/a}}).

Check with slopes: AB: 15 and DC: 6 − 58 − 3 = 15. AD: 42 = 2 and BC: 42 = 2.

Because {{a}}both pairs of opposite sides have equal slopes, ABCD with D(3, 5) is a parallelogram.{{/a}}

EX 15. Three vertices of parallelogram PQRS are P(0, 0), Q(5, 1), and R(7, 5). Find S.your turn
{{a}}

Step from Q to R: (2, 4). S = P + (2, 4) = (2, 4).

Summary
slopesdistancesperpendicularrhombusrectangleBecause

1. To show a right angle, show that two sides are {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}}: the product of their {{a}}slopes{{/a}} is −1.

2. To show a triangle is isosceles, compare side lengths found with {{a}}distances{{/a}}.

3. A parallelogram with a right angle is a {{a}}rectangle{{/a}}. A shape with four equal sides is a {{a}}rhombus{{/a}}.

4. A proof ends with a sentence that starts with "{{a}}Because{{/a}} ...".
