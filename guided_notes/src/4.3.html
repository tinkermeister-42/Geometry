4.3.1Parts of an Isosceles Triangle

  • An isosceles triangle has at least two congruent sides. They are the legs. The third side is the base.
  • The angle between the legs is the vertex angle.
  • The two angles that touch the base are the base angles.

4.3.2The Base Angles Theorem

  • Base angles theorem: In an isosceles triangle, the base angles are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.
  • Converse: If two angles of a triangle are congruent, the sides across from them are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.
  • Why it works: fold the triangle along the line from the vertex to the midpoint of the base. This is a {{a}}reflection{{/a}} (Unit 3). The two halves match, so ∠B lands on ∠C.
  • The vertex angle and the two base angles add to {{a}}180°{{/a}}.
{{fig 4.3_fold|isosceles triangle with the fold line from the vertex to the midpoint of the base|2.3in}}
EX 3. Find the base angles

The vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is 40°. Find each base angle.

The base angles are equal. Together they measure 180 − 40 = {{a}}140°{{/a}}.

Each base angle is 140 ÷ 2 = {{a}}70°{{/a}}.

EX 4. Find the vertex angle

A base angle of an isosceles triangle is 52°. Find the vertex angle.

Both base angles are 52°. Their sum is 2(52) = {{a}}104°{{/a}}.

The vertex angle is 180 − 104 = {{a}}76°{{/a}}.

4.3.3Equilateral Triangles

  • An equilateral triangle has three congruent sides. It is also equiangular: all three angles are congruent.
  • Each angle measures 180 ÷ 3 = {{a}}60°{{/a}}.
  • If all three angles of a triangle are 60°, the triangle is {{a}}equilateral{{/a}}.
EX 7. Equilateral with algebra
{{fig 4.3_equi|equilateral triangle with side labels 2x plus 1 and 3x minus 4|2.0in}}

△ ABC is equilateral. AB = 2x + 1 and BC = 3x − 4. Find x and the perimeter.

All sides are equal, so 2x + 1 = 3x − 4.

5 = x, so x = {{a}}5{{/a}}.

Each side is 2(5) + 1 = {{a}}11{{/a}}. The perimeter is 3 · 11 = {{a}}33{{/a}}.

4.3.4Solve for x in Isosceles Triangles

  • Equal sides: set the two sides {{a}}equal{{/a}}. Equal angles: set the two angles {{a}}equal{{/a}}.
  • Use the angle sum, {{a}}180°{{/a}}, when the vertex angle is also needed.
  • With two triangles that share a side, find the angles in one triangle at a time.
EX 9. Angles with x
{{fig 4.3_ang|isosceles triangle with a 50 degree vertex angle|2.0in}}

AB ≅ AC. The vertex angle is 50°. The base angle ∠B is (3x + 5)°. Find x.

Base angles together: 180 − 50 = 130. Each base angle is 130 ÷ 2 = {{a}}65{{/a}}.

3x + 5 = 65, so 3x = 60 and x = {{a}}20{{/a}}.

EX 10. Sides with x
{{fig 4.3_sides|isosceles triangle with side labels|1.9in}}

AB ≅ AC. AB = 2x + 1, AC = x + 8, and BC = 12. Find x and the perimeter.

The legs are equal, so 2x + 1 = x + 8 and x = {{a}}7{{/a}}.

Each leg is 7 + 8 = {{a}}15{{/a}}. The perimeter is 15 + 15 + 12 = {{a}}42{{/a}}.

EX 11. Two triangles that share a side
{{fig 4.3_two|two isosceles triangles that share the base AC|1.9in}}

AB ≅ BC and AD ≅ DC. m∠B = 40° and m∠D = 100°. Find {{mang BAD}}.

In △ ABC, each base angle is (180 − 40) ÷ 2 = {{a}}70°{{/a}}. So {{mang BAC}} = 70°.

In △ ADC, each base angle is (180 − 100) ÷ 2 = {{a}}40°{{/a}}. So {{mang CAD}} = 40°.

{{mang BAD}} = 70 + 40 = {{a}}110°{{/a}} (angle addition).

4.3.5Check on a Grid

  • In an isosceles triangle, the line from the vertex angle to the midpoint of the base is {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}} to the base.
  • To check: find the {{a}}midpoint{{/a}} of the base. Then find the slopes. Slopes of perpendicular lines multiply to {{a}}−1{{/a}}.
EX 13. Show it with slopes
{{fig 4.3_grid|isosceles triangle ABC on a grid with the segment from A to the midpoint M of BC|2.3in}}

A(7, 2), B(3, 5), C(7, 7). Show that △ ABC is isosceles with base {{seg BC}}. Show that the line from A to the midpoint M of {{seg BC}} is perpendicular to {{seg BC}}.

AB = 42 + 32 = {{a}}5{{/a}} and AC = 02 + 52 = {{a}}5{{/a}}. The legs are equal.

Midpoint of {{seg BC}}: M = {{a}}(5, 6){{/a}}

Slope of {{seg BC}}: {{f 7 − 5/7 − 3}} = {{a}}{{f 1/2}}{{/a}}

Slope of {{seg AM}}: {{f 6 − 2/5 − 7}} = {{a}}−2{{/a}}

{{f 1/2}} · (−2) = −1, so {{seg AM}} ⊥ {{seg BC}}.

Summary
base anglescongruentequilateralperpendicularreflectionvertex angle

1. The two congruent sides of an isosceles triangle are the legs. The angle between them is the {{a}}vertex angle{{/a}}. The angles on the base are the {{a}}base angles{{/a}}.

2. Base angles are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}. The converse is also true: equal angles mean equal sides. A fold line is a {{a}}reflection{{/a}}.

3. An {{a}}equilateral{{/a}} triangle has three 60° angles.

4. The line from the vertex to the midpoint of the base is {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}} to the base.
