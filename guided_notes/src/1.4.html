1.4.1Parallel, Perpendicular, and Skew Lines

  • Parallel lines are in the same plane and never {{a}}meet{{/a}}. Symbol: {{a}}∥{{/a}}. Matching {{a}}arrows{{/a}} mark them.
  • Perpendicular lines cross to make {{a}}right angles{{/a}}. Symbol: {{a}}⊥{{/a}}.
  • Skew lines are not in the same {{a}}plane{{/a}}. They do not meet and are not parallel.

A transversal is a line that {{a}}crosses{{/a}} two or more other lines. It makes {{a}}8{{/a}} angles. Angles between the two lines are {{a}}interior{{/a}}. Angles outside are {{a}}exterior{{/a}}.

1.4.3Parallel Lines and the Eight Angles

When the lines are parallel

Corresponding angles are {{a}}equal{{/a}}. Alternate interior angles are {{a}}equal{{/a}}. Alternate exterior angles are {{a}}equal{{/a}}.

Same-side interior angles are {{a}}supplementary{{/a}} (they add to {{a}}180°{{/a}}).

1.4.4Are the Lines Parallel?

The rules work backward. If a transversal makes equal {{a}}corresponding{{/a}} angles, equal alternate interior angles, equal alternate exterior angles, or same-side interior angles that add to {{a}}180°{{/a}}, then the lines are {{a}}parallel{{/a}}.
EX 12. The lines look parallel. The marked angles are corresponding. Are ℓ and m parallel?
{{fig 1.4_converse|lines l and m cut by transversal t; corresponding angles marked 88 degrees and 92 degrees|3.8in}}

88° {{a}}≠{{/a}} 92°. The corresponding angles are {{a}}not equal{{/a}}, so the lines are {{a}}not parallel{{/a}}.

Summary
180°equalparallelperpendicularskewtransversal

1. Lines that cross at right angles are {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}}. Lines in space that never meet and are not parallel are {{a}}skew{{/a}}.

2. A {{a}}transversal{{/a}} crosses two lines and makes 8 angles.

3. If the lines are {{a}}parallel{{/a}}, corresponding and alternate angles are {{a}}equal{{/a}}.

4. If the lines are parallel, same-side interior angles add to {{a}}180°{{/a}}.

5. We trust marks and measures, not how the lines look.
