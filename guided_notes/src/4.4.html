4.4.1The Quadrilateral Family

  • A quadrilateral has 4 sides. Its 4 angles add to {{a}}360°{{/a}}.
  • Trapezoid: exactly one pair of parallel sides. Kite: two pairs of congruent sides that are next to each other.
  • Parallelogram: both pairs of opposite sides are {{a}}parallel{{/a}}.
  • Rectangle: a parallelogram with 4 {{a}}right{{/a}} angles. Rhombus: a parallelogram with 4 {{a}}congruent{{/a}} sides.
  • Square: a rectangle and a rhombus. It has 4 right angles and 4 congruent sides.

4.4.2Parallelograms

  • Opposite sides of a parallelogram are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.
  • Opposite angles are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.
  • Consecutive angles (angles next to each other) are {{a}}supplementary{{/a}}. They add to 180°.
  • The diagonals {{a}}bisect{{/a}} each other. They meet at the midpoint of each diagonal.
  • The converse works too: if the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, it is a parallelogram.
EX 3. Find x and y
{{fig 4.4_para|parallelogram ABCD with algebra labels|2.8in}}

In parallelogram ABCD, AB = 2x + 3, CD = 4x − 9, m∠A = (5y − 10)°, and m∠C = 70°. Find x, y, and m∠B.

Opposite sides are equal: 2x + 3 = 4x − 9. So 12 = 2x and x = {{a}}6{{/a}}.

Opposite angles are equal: 5y − 10 = 70. So 5y = 80 and y = {{a}}16{{/a}}.

∠B and ∠A are consecutive: m∠B = 180 − 70 = {{a}}110°{{/a}}.

4.4.3Rectangles, Rhombuses, and Squares

  • A rectangle has all the properties of a parallelogram. Its diagonals are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.
  • A rhombus has all the properties of a parallelogram. Its diagonals are {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}}, and each diagonal {{a}}bisects{{/a}} a pair of opposite angles.
  • A square has all of these properties. Its diagonals are congruent and perpendicular.
EX 6. Rectangle diagonals
{{fig 4.4_rect|rectangle with both diagonals labeled|2.3in}}

ABCD is a rectangle. AC = 3x + 2 and BD = 5x − 12. Find x and AC.

The diagonals of a rectangle are congruent: 3x + 2 = 5x − 12.

14 = 2x, so x = {{a}}7{{/a}}.

AC = 3(7) + 2 = {{a}}23{{/a}}

EX 7. Rhombus angles
{{fig 4.4_rhomb|rhombus ABCD with diagonals meeting at E|2.9in}}

ABCD is a rhombus. {{mang CAB}} = 28°. Find {{mang DAB}}, {{mang ABC}}, and {{mang AEB}}.

Diagonal {{seg AC}} bisects {{ang DAB}}: {{mang DAB}} = 2(28) = {{a}}56°{{/a}}.

Consecutive angles add to 180°: {{mang ABC}} = 180 − 56 = {{a}}124°{{/a}}.

The diagonals are perpendicular: {{mang AEB}} = {{a}}90°{{/a}}.

Is the frame square?
A builder measures both diagonals of a frame. If the opposite sides match and the diagonals match, the frame is a rectangle. The corners are square.

4.4.4Trapezoids and Kites

  • A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides, the bases. The other two sides are the legs.
  • The midsegment of a trapezoid connects the midpoints of the legs. It is {{a}}parallel{{/a}} to the bases. Its length is the {{a}}average{{/a}} of the bases: {{f b1 + b2/2}}.
  • Isosceles trapezoid: the legs are congruent. Each pair of base angles is {{a}}congruent{{/a}}. The diagonals are congruent.
  • Kite: the diagonals are {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}}. One pair of opposite angles is congruent (the angles between the unequal sides).
EX 9. Midsegment
{{fig 4.4_trap|trapezoid with a dashed midsegment|2.2in}}

The bases of a trapezoid are 12 and 28. The midsegment is (3x + 5). Find x.

Midsegment = {{f 12 + 28/2}} = {{a}}20{{/a}}.

3x + 5 = 20, so 3x = 15 and x = {{a}}5{{/a}}.

EX 10. Isosceles trapezoid
{{fig 4.4_isotrap|isosceles trapezoid with a 70 degree angle|2.2in}}

ABCD is an isosceles trapezoid with {{seg AB}} ∥ {{seg DC}}. m∠D = 70°. Find m∠C, m∠A, and m∠B.

Base angles are congruent: m∠C = {{a}}70°{{/a}}.

∠A and ∠D are consecutive angles between parallel lines: m∠A = 180 − 70 = {{a}}110°{{/a}}.

m∠B = {{a}}110°{{/a}}

EX 11. Kite
{{fig 4.4_kite|kite ABCD with perpendicular diagonals|2.2in}}

ABCD is a kite. AB = AD and CB = CD. m∠A = 80° and m∠B = 110°. Find m∠D and m∠C.

The angles between the unequal sides are congruent: m∠D = {{a}}110°{{/a}}.

The four angles add to 360°: m∠C = 360 − 80 − 110 − 110 = {{a}}60°{{/a}}.

4.4.5Always, Sometimes, or Never?

  • A shape is {{a}}always{{/a}} a member of every group above it on the family tree.
  • To show a statement is not always true, find a {{a}}counterexample{{/a}}: one example where it fails.
  • Use one of the four ways to justify: a drawing to scale, a coordinate check, a counterexample, or a mark on a figure.
EX 13. Decide and explain

a. A square is a rectangle. {{a}}Always{{/a}}. A square has 4 right angles and opposite sides parallel.

b. A rectangle is a rhombus. {{a}}Sometimes{{/a}}. A rectangle 2 by 5 is a counterexample: the sides are not all equal. A square is a rectangle that is also a rhombus.

c. A trapezoid is a parallelogram. {{a}}Never{{/a}}. A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides. A parallelogram has two.

Summary
bisectcongruentdiagonalsparallelperpendicularsupplementary

1. A parallelogram has {{a}}parallel{{/a}} opposite sides, congruent opposite angles, {{a}}supplementary{{/a}} consecutive angles, and diagonals that {{a}}bisect{{/a}} each other.

2. A rectangle has congruent {{a}}diagonals{{/a}}. A rhombus has {{a}}perpendicular{{/a}} diagonals.

3. A trapezoid midsegment is parallel to the bases. Its length is the average of the bases. In an isosceles trapezoid the diagonals are {{a}}congruent{{/a}}.

4. A counterexample shows a statement is not always true.
