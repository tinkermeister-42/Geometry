8.1.1Parallelograms, Triangles, and Trapezoids

  • Area counts the {{a}}square units{{/a}} that cover a flat shape.
  • The height is measured at a {{a}}right angle{{/a}} to the base. It is not the {{a}}slanted side{{/a}}.
  • Parallelogram: cut off a triangle and move it. You get a {{a}}rectangle{{/a}}, so A = {{a}}bh{{/a}}.
  • Two triangles make a parallelogram, so A = {{a}}{{f 1/2}}bh{{/a}}.
  • Two trapezoids make a parallelogram with base b1 + b2, so A = {{a}}{{f 1/2}}(b1 + b2)h{{/a}}.

8.1.2Regular Polygons

  • A regular polygon has equal {{a}}sides{{/a}} and equal {{a}}angles{{/a}}.
  • The apothem a goes from the {{a}}center{{/a}} to the {{a}}middle of a side{{/a}}, at a right angle.
  • Cut the polygon into triangles from the center. Each has base = a side and height = {{a}}a{{/a}}.
  • A = {{a}}{{f 1/2}}aP{{/a}}, where P is the {{a}}perimeter{{/a}}.
EX 10. A regular hexagon has side 6 in. Find its area. Give the exact answer and a decimal rounded to the nearest tenth.
{{fig 8.1_hex|a regular hexagon with side 6 inches, split into six triangles from the center, with the apothem drawn|2.6in}}

The center and a side make an equilateral triangle. The apothem cuts it into two {{a}}30°-60°-90° triangles{{/a}}.

Short leg = {{a}}3{{/a}}, so a = {{a}}3√3{{/a}}

P = 6 · 6 = {{a}}36{{/a}}

A = {{f 1/2}}aP = {{a}}{{f 1/2}}(3√3)(36) = 54√3 ≈ 93.5 square inches{{/a}}

8.1.3Composite Figures

A composite figure is made of simple shapes. Two ways to find its area:
  • {{a}}Add{{/a}} the areas of the parts.
  • {{a}}Subtract{{/a}} a missing part from a bigger shape.
