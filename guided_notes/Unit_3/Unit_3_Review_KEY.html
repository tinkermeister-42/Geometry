Teacher Key: answers in red

Worked Examples

EX 1. 3.1 Translate P(−3, 4) by (x + 5, y − 6). Then find the rule that moves A(2, −1) to A′(−4, 3).

P′ = (2, −2). Change in x: −4 − 2 = −6. Change in y: 3 − (−1) = 4. Rule: (x − 6, y + 4).

EX 2. 3.2 Reflect (−4, 3) over the y-axis and over y = x. Find the line of reflection that maps A(0, 2) to A′(6, 2).

y-axis: (4, 3). y = x: (3, −4). The midpoint of AA' is (3, 2) and AA' is horizontal, so the line is x = 3.

EX 3. 3.3 Rotate (3, −2) by 90° and by 180° counterclockwise about the origin. Find the rotation that maps (−1, −5) to (−5, 1).

90°: (−y, x) = (2, 3). 180°: (−3, 2). For (−1, −5) → (−5, 1) the rule (y, −x) works, so it is 270° counterclockwise (90° clockwise).

EX 4. 3.4 Dilate (6, −9) with k = 13 about the origin. A side of 8 becomes 20. Find k. Dilate B(5, 3) with k = 2 about (1, 1).

(2, −3). k = 208 = 52. B: (4, 2) → (8, 4) → (9, 5).

EX 5. 3.5 Do "translate by (x + 2, y), then reflect over the y-axis" on P(1, 3). Do the moves in the other order. Then give the order of rotational symmetry and the smallest angle for a regular octagon.

First order: (3, 3) → (−3, 3). Other order: (−1, 3) → (1, 3). Different. A regular octagon has order 8 and smallest angle 360°8 = 45°.

Practice

3.1 - I can translate a figure, write a translation rule, and check it with distance and slope

1. Apply (x − 6, y + 3) to (4, −5).

(−2, −2)

2. A(−3, 2) moves to A′(1, −4). Write the rule and say it in words.

(x + 4, y − 6): 4 right and 6 down.

3. Triangle RST has R(0, 0), S(4, 0), T(0, 3). Translate it by (x + 2, y + 5). Give the image coordinates.

R′(2, 5), S′(6, 5), T′(2, 8)

4. Show that ST = S′T′ and that the slopes are equal for the triangle in problem 3.

ST = 5 and S′T′ = 5. Both slopes are −34.

5. The image of (3a, a + 2) under (x − 5, y + 1) is (4, 6). Find a.

3a − 5 = 4, so a = 3. Check: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.

3.2 - I can reflect a figure over a line and find the line of reflection

6. Reflect (−5, 2) over the x-axis.

(−5, −2)

7. Reflect (3, −6) over the line y = −x.

(6, −3)

8. Reflect (1, 4) over the line x = −2.

(−5, 4)

9. A(−2, 3) is reflected to A′(4, 3). Find the line of reflection.

The midpoint is (1, 3) and AA' is horizontal. The line is x = 1.

10. Triangle ABC has A(1, 1), B(5, 2), C(2, 4). Reflect it over the x-axis. Does the orientation stay the same?

A′(1, −1), B′(5, −2), C′(2, −4). No. A reflection reverses orientation.

3.3 - I can rotate a figure about the origin and find a rotation

11. Rotate (−4, 1) by 90° counterclockwise about the origin.

(−1, −4)

12. Rotate (2, −7) by 180° about the origin.

(−2, 7)

13. Rotate (5, 3) by 90° clockwise about the origin.

(3, −5)

14. (4, −2) → (2, 4) about the origin. Find the rotation.

(−y, x) = (2, 4). It is 90° counterclockwise.

15. A(3, 4) rotates 90° counterclockwise to A′(−4, 3). Show OA = OA′ and OA ⊥ OA'.

OA = 5 and OA′ = 5. The slopes are 43 and −34. Their product is −1.

3.4 - I can dilate a figure and find a scale factor

16. Dilate (−4, 6) with k = 12 about the origin.

(−2, 3)

17. Dilate (3, −2) with k = 4 about the origin.

(12, −8)

18. A(2, 6) → A′(5, 15) about the origin. Find k.

k = 52

19. Dilate (4, 4) with k = 3 about the center (2, 3).

(8, 6)

20. A triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 is dilated with k = 1.5. Find the new sides. What happens to the angles?

9, 12, 15. The angles stay the same.

3.5 - I can do a sequence of transformations and find symmetry

21. Translate (2, 3) by (x + 3, y − 2). Then reflect over the x-axis.

(5, −1)

22. Reflect over the y-axis, then translate by (x + 4, y + 1). Write one rule.

(x, y) → (−x + 4, y + 1)

23. Use P(1, 2). Show that "rotate 90° counterclockwise, then translate by (x + 3, y)" is not the same as "translate first, then rotate".

First way: (−2, 1) → (1, 1). Other way: (4, 2) → (−2, 4). Different.

24. Find the lines of symmetry, the order of rotational symmetry, and the smallest angle for a regular hexagon.

6 lines, order 6, 60°

25. Find the lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of the letter S.

0 lines, order 2
