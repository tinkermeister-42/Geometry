3.5.1One After Another

  • A sequence of transformations does one transformation after another.
  • A → A′→ A′′. Always do the moves in the order given.
  • A combined rule shows the whole sequence in one rule.
EX 1. Do a sequence

Translate P(2, 5) by (x + 3, y − 2). Then reflect the image over the y-axis.

Step 1: P′ = (2 + 3, 5 − 2) = (5, 3)

Step 2: P′′ = (−5, 3), so P′′ = (−5, 3)

Combined rule: (x, y) → (−x − 3, y − 2). Check: (−2 − 3, 5 − 2) = (−5, 3).

EX 2. Try ityour turn

a. Reflect Q(−4, 1) over the x-axis. Then translate by (x + 6, y + 2). Find Q′′.

Q′ = (−4, −1). Q′′ = (2, 1).

b. Translate by (x + 2, y + 5). Then translate by (x − 7, y − 1). Write one rule.

(x + 2 − 7, y + 5 − 1) = (x − 5, y + 4)

3.5.2Does Order Matter?

  • Two translations in either order give the same image.
  • A reflection and a translation, in a different order, often give a different image.
  • One example that gives different images is a counterexample to the claim "order never matters."
EX 3. A counterexample

Use P(2, 1). Do these two moves in both orders: reflect over the x-axis, and translate by (x + 4, y − 3).

Reflect first: (2, −1). Then translate: (6, −4).

Translate first: (6, −2). Then reflect: (6, 2).

The images are different. So order matters here.

EX 4. See it on a triangle

A(1, 2), B(3, 2), C(1, 4). Reflect over the x-axis, then translate by (x + 4, y − 3). This gives A1, B1, C1. Do the moves in the other order to get A2, B2, C2.

A1 = (5, −5), B1 = (7, −5), C1 = (5, −7)

A2 = (5, 1), B2 = (7, 1), C2 = (5, −1)

EX 5. Use R(3, 4). Is "translate by (x + 2, y), then reflect over the y-axis" the same as "reflect over the y-axis, then translate by (x + 2, y)"?your turn

First way: (5, 4) → (−5, 4). Second way: (−3, 4) → (−1, 4). They are not the same.

3.5.3Describing a Sequence

  • Look at the orientation. If it reversed, the sequence needs a reflection.
  • Compare sizes. If the size changed, the sequence needs a dilation.
  • Check your sequence with one or two vertices.
EX 6. Describe a sequence

△ ABC with A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(1, 3) maps to △ A′′B′′C′′. Describe a sequence.

The labels go the other way around, so the sequence includes a reflection.

Reflect over the y-axis: A′ = (−1, 1). Then translate: A′′ = (−1, −4), which is 5 down: (x, y − 5).

Another answer: translate 5 down first, then reflect over the y-axis. It gives the same △ A′′B′′C′′.

EX 7. D(2, 3), E(5, 3), F(2, 5) map to D′′(−2, 4), E′′(−2, 7), F′′(−4, 4). Describe a sequence. Hint: try a rotation first.your turn

Rotate 90° counterclockwise about the origin: D′(−3, 2). Then translate (x + 1, y + 2): D′′(−2, 4). Check E: (−3, 5) → (−2, 7).

3.5.4Rigid Motions and Dilations

TransformationKeeps lengths?Keeps angles?Rigid motion?
translationyesyesyes
reflectionyesyesyes
rotationyesyesyes
dilation (k ≠ 1)noyesno
  • Any sequence of rigid motions is a rigid motion. It keeps lengths and angles.
  • A dilation changes lengths. It keeps angles.
EX 8. Choose the transformation

Which transformation fits each description?

a. Lengths and angles stay the same. The figure is flipped. reflection

b. All lengths double. Angles stay the same. dilation

c. Lengths and angles stay the same. The figure is only moved over. translation

EX 9. Triangle ABC has sides 3, 4, 5. Triangle A′B′C′ has sides 6, 8, 10 and the same angles. Could a sequence of rigid motions map ABC onto A′B′C′? Explain.your turn

No. Rigid motions keep lengths. A dilation with k = 2 is needed.

3.5.5Symmetry

  • Line symmetry: a reflection over a line maps the figure onto itself. The line is a line of symmetry.
  • Rotational symmetry: a rotation of less than 360° maps the figure onto itself.
  • The order n is the number of times it fits in one full turn. The smallest angle is 360°n.
EX 10. Symmetry of letters

Find the lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry.

H: 2 lines (vertical and horizontal). Turn 180° and it fits. Order 2.

S: 0 lines. Turn 180° and it fits. Order 2.

A: 1 line (vertical). No turn less than 360° fits. Order 1.

EX 11. Regular polygons

A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order n (see 1.5).

Equilateral triangle: 3 lines, order 3, smallest angle 3603 = 120°.

Square: 4 lines, order 4, smallest angle 3604 = 90°.

Regular pentagon: 5 lines, order 5, smallest angle 3605 = 72°.

EX 12. Find the order and the smallest angle of rotation for a regular hexagon. How many lines of symmetry does it have?your turn

Order 6, smallest angle 3606 = 60°, and 6 lines of symmetry.

A rectangle is not a square
A rectangle that is not a square has 2 lines of symmetry, not 4. Folding it along a diagonal does not make the halves match.
Summary
dilationdoubleorderreflectionrigidsymmetry

1. After two moves, A becomes A′′, read "A double prime".

2. Two moves in a different order can give different images.

3. A sequence of rigid motions is a rigid motion. A dilation is not.

4. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and its smallest rotation is 360°n.

13. Use P(2, 5). Show that "translate by (x + 3, y), then reflect over the y-axis" is different from "reflect over the y-axis, then translate by (x + 3, y)".

First way: (5, 5) → (−5, 5). Second way: (−2, 5) → (1, 5). They are different.

14. True or false: two translations in either order give the same image. Explain.

True. Both orders add the same numbers to x and to y.

15. Diego says "reflect over the x-axis, then over the y-axis" is the same as a 180° rotation about the origin. Test it with (3, −2).

(3, −2) → (3, 2) → (−3, 2). A 180° rotation gives (−3, 2). Diego is right for this point.

Describe a sequence.

16. A(1, 2), B(4, 2), C(1, 5) map to A′′(−1, −2), B′′(−4, −2), C′′(−1, −5). Find one transformation that does it.

A rotation of 180° about the origin: (−x, −y). (Reflecting over the x-axis and then the y-axis also works.)

17. D(1, 1), E(3, 1), F(1, 4) map to D′′(5, 0), E′′(7, 0), F′′(5, −3). Describe a sequence.

Reflect over the x-axis, then translate by (x + 4, y + 1). Check F: (1, −4) → (5, −3).

18. Describe a different sequence for problem 17.

Reflect over the line y = 12, then translate by (x + 4, y). Check F: (1, −3) → (5, −3).

Classify the transformation.

19. A transformation keeps all lengths and angles. Which of these could it be: translation, reflection, rotation, dilation?

Translation, reflection, or rotation. These are the rigid motions.

20. A triangle with sides 3, 4, 5 maps to a triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 and the same angles. Which transformation is it? Is it a rigid motion?

A dilation with k = 2. It is not a rigid motion.

21. True or false: every dilation is a rigid motion. Explain.

False. A dilation with k = 2 doubles lengths. Only k = 1 keeps lengths.

33. Reflect P(3, 2) over the line x = 1. Then reflect the image over the line x = 4. Find P′′. Compare P and P′′. What single translation does this equal? How does its size relate to the distance between the two lines?

x = 1: (−1, 2). x = 4: (2 · 4 − (−1), 2) = (9, 2). P′′ = (9, 2) is 6 right of P. So the two reflections equal (x + 6, y). The lines are 3 apart, and 6 = 2 · 3.
