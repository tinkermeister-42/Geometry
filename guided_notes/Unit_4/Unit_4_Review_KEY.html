Unit Review - Unit 4: Congruence - DHS Geometry
Teacher Key: answers in red

Worked Examples

EX 1. 4.1 △ ABC ≅ △ DEF. AB = 3x − 4 and DE = 17. m∠C = 2y + 10 and m∠F = 70°. Find x and y. Which side matches BC?

3x − 4 = 17, so x = 7. 2y + 10 = 70, so y = 30. BC matches EF.

EX 2. 4.2 AB = 7, BC = 9, m∠B = 40°. DE = 7, EF = 9, m∠E = 40°. Are the triangles congruent? Then try PQ = 5, QR = 8, m∠P = 30° and ST = 5, TU = 8, m∠S = 30°.

The first pair is congruent by SAS. The angle is between the two sides. In the second pair the angle is not between the sides. That is SSA, which is not enough. We cannot tell.

EX 3. 4.3 AB = AC in △ ABC. m∠B = (3x + 5)° and m∠C = (5x − 15)°. Find x and m∠A.

Base angles are equal: 3x + 5 = 5x − 15, so x = 10. Each base angle is 35°. m∠A = 180 − 35 − 35 = 110°.

EX 4. 4.4 In parallelogram ABCD, m∠A = (2x + 20)° and m∠B = (3x − 10)°. Find x and the four angles.

Consecutive angles add to 180°: 5x + 10 = 180, so x = 34. m∠A = 88° and m∠B = 92°. Opposite angles are equal, so m∠C = 88° and m∠D = 92°.

EX 5. 4.5 List the steps to bisect ∠A. Say why the construction works. If m∠A = 84°, how large is each new angle?

Draw an arc across both sides, at P and Q. Draw equal arcs from P and from Q that cross at R. Draw AR. It works because △ APR ≅ △ AQR by SSS. Each new angle is 42°.

Practice

4.1 − I can name matching parts, find missing parts, and use rigid motions.

1. △ PQR ≅ △ XYZ. Name the angle that matches ∠Q and the side that matches PR.

∠Y and XZ

2. △ LMN ≅ △ STU, LM = 5x + 2, and ST = 4x + 9. Find x and LM.

5x + 2 = 4x + 9, so x = 7. LM = 37.

3. △ ABC has A(1, 2), B(4, 2), C(1, 6). Translate it by (x + 5, y − 3). Give the image. Show AC = A′C′.

A′(6, −1), B′(9, −1), C′(6, 3). AC = 4 and A′C′ = 4.

4. △ ABC ≅ △ DEF, m∠A = 4y − 8, and m∠D = 52°. Find y.

4y − 8 = 52, so y = 15.

5. Maria says △ ABC ≅ △ DEF means AB matches DF. Find the mistake.

The order of the letters shows the matching. A is first and D is first, B is second and E is second. So AB matches DE.

4.2 − I can decide which rule shows two triangles are congruent.

6. Two triangles have sides 4, 6, 8. Which rule shows they are congruent?

SSS

7. m∠A = 50°, m∠B = 60°, AB = 9 and m∠D = 50°, m∠E = 60°, DE = 9. Which rule?

ASA. The side is between the two angles.

8. Two right triangles each have a hypotenuse of 13 and a leg of 5. Which rule?

HL

9. Two triangles each have angles 40° and 70° and a side of 8 that is not between them. Which rule?

AAS

10. A 3, 4, 5 triangle and a 6, 8, 10 triangle both have a right angle and a 37° angle. Are they congruent? What does this show?

No. The sides are different. The angles match, so AAA is not enough.

11. A(0, 0), B(4, 0), C(0, 3) and D(1, 1), E(5, 1), F(1, 4). Show △ ABC ≅ △ DEF.

AB = 4 = DE and AC = 3 = DF. Each triangle has a right angle between those sides (horizontal and vertical). They are congruent by SAS.

4.3 − I can use isosceles and equilateral triangles.

12. The vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is 52°. Find each base angle.

180 − 522 = 64°

13. A base angle of an isosceles triangle is 25°. Find the vertex angle.

180 − 25 − 25 = 130°

14. An equilateral triangle has sides 2x + 7, 3x − 2, and the third side. Find x and the perimeter.

2x + 7 = 3x − 2, so x = 9. Each side is 25. The perimeter is 75.

15. A triangle has angles 70°, 70°, and 40°. Which two sides are congruent?

The sides opposite the two 70° angles.

16. A(0, 0), B(8, 0), C(4, 6). Show the triangle is isosceles.

AC = 16 + 36 = 52 and BC = 16 + 36 = 52. Two sides are equal.

17. The exterior angle at a base vertex of an isosceles triangle is 130°. Find the three angles of the triangle.

Base angle: 180 − 130 = 50°. The other base angle is 50°. The vertex angle is 80°.

4.4 − I can use the properties of quadrilaterals.

18. Opposite sides are parallel, all four sides are congruent, and there are no right angles. Name the quadrilateral.

rhombus

19. In parallelogram ABCD, m∠A = 115°. Find the other three angles.

m∠B = 65°, m∠C = 115°, m∠D = 65°.

20. The diagonals of a parallelogram meet at E. AE = 2x + 3 and EC = 4x − 7. Find x and AC.

2x + 3 = 4x − 7, so x = 5. AE = 13 and AC = 26.

21. P(0, 0), Q(5, 1), R(7, 5), S(2, 4). Is PQRS a parallelogram? Use the diagonals.

Midpoint of PR: (3.5, 2.5). Midpoint of QS: (3.5, 2.5). Same, so yes.

22. The bases of a trapezoid are 14 and 26. Find the midsegment.

14 + 262 = 20

23. The diagonals of a rectangle are 5x − 4 and 3x + 10. Find x and the diagonal.

5x − 4 = 3x + 10, so x = 7. The diagonal is 31.

24. Always, sometimes, or never: a square is a rhombus. A parallelogram is a rectangle.

A square is always a rhombus. A parallelogram is sometimes a rectangle. A parallelogram with no right angles is a counterexample.

4.5 − I can make constructions with a compass and a straightedge.

25. Draw a segment 6 cm long. Construct its perpendicular bisector. Label the midpoint M.

AM = 3 cm. The arcs cross above and below. The line through them is perpendicular to the segment.

26. Draw an angle of about 70°. Bisect it. Measure each part to check.

Each part is about 35°.

27. Draw a line and a point P not on it. Construct the line through P perpendicular to the line.

The line meets the first line at a right angle.

28. Construct an equilateral triangle with side 5 cm. Measure one angle.

Each angle is 60°.

29. P is on the perpendicular bisector of AB. PA = 2x + 9 and PB = 4x − 1. Find x and PA.

PA = PB, so 2x + 9 = 4x − 1 and x = 5. PA = 19.

30. A regular hexagon is built in a circle with radius 4 cm. Find the perimeter.

Each side equals the radius: 6 · 4 = 24 cm.
