4.2.1Why Triangles? SSS

  • A triangle with three fixed side lengths can have only one shape. A four-sided frame can tilt.
  • SSS (side, side, side): If three sides of one triangle are congruent to three sides of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.
  • SSS is the rule behind the distance check on a grid in 4.1.
EX 1. Use SSS

Are the triangles congruent? Write a congruence statement.

The marks show AB ≅ YZ (one tick), BC ≅ ZX (two ticks), and AC ≅ YX (three ticks).

All three pairs of sides are congruent. By SSS, △ ABC ≅ △ YZX.

The letters follow the marks: A matches Y, B matches Z, and C matches X.

EX 2. △ PQR has sides PQ = 6, QR = 8, PR = 10. △ STU has sides ST = 6, TU = 8, SU = 10. Are the triangles congruent? Which rule?your turn

Yes, by SSS. △ PQR ≅ △ STU.

Triangles hold their shape
A bridge truss, a roof truss, and a bike frame are made of triangles. A triangle cannot be pushed out of shape. A rectangle can.

4.2.2SAS, ASA, AAS, and HL

  • SAS (side, angle, side): two sides and the angle between them. The angle between two sides is the included angle.
  • ASA (angle, side, angle): two angles and the side between them. The side between two angles is the included side.
  • AAS (angle, angle, side): two angles and a side that is not between them.
  • HL (hypotenuse, leg): works for right triangles only. The hypotenuse and one leg are congruent.
EX 3. Use SAS

The marks show AB ≅ DE, AC ≅ DF, and ∠A ≅ ∠D.

Angle A is between AB and AC. Angle D is between DE and DF.

Two sides and the included angle match. By SAS, △ ABC ≅ △ DEF.

EX 4. Use AAS

The marks show ∠G ≅ ∠J, ∠H ≅ ∠K, and HI ≅ KL.

Side HI is not between ∠G and ∠H. It touches ∠H and ∠I.

Two angles and a side that is not between them match. By AAS, △ GHI ≅ △ JKL.

EX 5. Use HL

The figure shows right angles at A and D. The hypotenuses BC and EF are congruent. The legs AC and DF are congruent.

Both triangles are right triangles, and the hypotenuse and a leg match. By HL, △ ABC ≅ △ DEF.

EX 6. Try ityour turn

a. In △ ABC and △ DEF, ∠A ≅ ∠D, AB ≅ DE, and ∠B ≅ ∠E. Which rule shows the triangles are congruent?

ASA. Side AB is between ∠A and ∠B.

b. In △ ABC and △ DEF, AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, and ∠B ≅ ∠E. Which rule?

SAS. Angle B is between AB and BC.

4.2.3What Is Not Enough: SSA and AAA

  • SSA (two sides and an angle that is not between them) is not enough. Two different triangles can have these parts.
  • AAA (three angles) is not enough. The triangles have the same shape but can have different sizes.
  • A counterexample is one example that shows a rule does not always work.
EX 7. A counterexample for SSA

In the figure, AB = 5, BC = BD = 3.2, and ∠A = 30° in both triangles. Two sides and a non-included angle match.

But AC is about 2.3 and AD is about 6.3. The triangles are not congruent.

So SSA does not show that triangles are congruent.

EX 8. A counterexample for AAA

Both triangles have angles 40°, 60°, and 80°. The larger triangle has sides that are 2 times as long.

The angles match, but the sizes are different. The triangles are not congruent. AAA does not work.

EX 9. Two right triangles both have angles 30°, 60°, and 90°. One has a hypotenuse of 10 and the other has a hypotenuse of 20. Are they congruent? Why?your turn

No. The angles match (AAA), but AAA is not enough. The sizes are different.

Not enough does not mean not congruent
If the marks give only SSA or AAA, the triangles might be congruent. You just cannot tell from those facts. Write “not enough information”.

4.2.4Which Shortcut? Use the Marks and the Given Facts

  • Use only the marks and the facts you are given. A picture can look congruent and not be.
  • A side that both triangles share is congruent to itself. It counts as one pair of sides.
  • Vertical angles are equal (1.3). Parallel lines make equal alternate interior angles (1.4).
  • Write the congruence statement with the letters in matching order. If no rule fits, write “not enough information”.
EX 10. A shared side

AB ≅ AD and BC ≅ DC. Are △ ABC and △ ADC congruent?

Both triangles share AC, so AC ≅ AC.

Three pairs of sides are congruent. By SSS, △ ABC ≅ △ ADC.

EX 11. Vertical angles

AD and BC cross at E. AE ≅ DE and BE ≅ CE. Which rule shows △ AEB ≅ △ DEC?

∠AEB and ∠DEC are vertical angles, so they are equal.

Two sides and the included angle match. By SAS, △ AEB ≅ △ DEC.

EX 12. Parallel lines

AB ∥ DC and AB ≅ DC. Which rule shows △ ABD ≅ △ CDB?

BD is a shared side. Because AB ∥ DC, the alternate interior angles ∠ABD and ∠CDB are equal.

Two sides and the included angle match. By SAS, △ ABD ≅ △ CDB.

EX 13. Not enough information

The triangles look the same. The marks show AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, and ∠A ≅ ∠D.

Angle A is not between AB and BC. This is SSA.

SSA is not enough. The answer is: not enough information.

EX 14. PQ and RS cross at T. PT ≅ ST and QT ≅ RT. Which rule shows △ PTQ ≅ △ STR?your turn

The angles at T are vertical angles, so they are equal. Two sides and the included angle match: SAS.

4.2.5Solve for x in Congruent Triangles

  • First use the marks to decide that the triangles are congruent.
  • Then matching parts are congruent. Set them equal and solve.
EX 15. Find x, y, and z

AC ≅ DF, BC ≅ EF, and ∠C ≅ ∠F. Find x, y, z, and AB.

Two sides and the included angle match. By SAS, △ ABC ≅ △ DEF.

BC = EF, so 2y + 3 = 15 and y = 6.

∠C ≅ ∠F, so 5z + 1 = 61 and z = 12.

AB matches DE, so 3x + 1 = 5x − 9. Then 10 = 2x and x = 5.

AB = 3(5) + 1 = 16

EX 16. △ ABC ≅ △ DEF by SSS. AC = 4x − 7, DF = 2x + 9, BC = y + 5, and EF = 3y − 7. Find x, y, and BC.your turn

4x − 7 = 2x + 9, so x = 8. y + 5 = 3y − 7, so y = 6. BC = 11.

Summary
betweencongruentcounterexamplehypotenusenot enoughright

1. Two triangles are congruent if SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or HL is true.

2. SAS needs the angle between the two sides. ASA needs the side between the two angles. HL needs a right triangle: hypotenuse and a leg.

3. SSA and AAA are not enough. One counterexample shows a rule does not work.

4. Use only marks and given facts. A shared side, vertical angles, and parallel lines can give extra pairs.

Challenge

28. A(0, 0), B(6, 0), and C(2, 4). Find the point D above AB so that △ BAD ≅ △ ABC with D matching C. Check with SSS.

D = (4, 4) (reflect C over x = 3). AD = 16 + 16 = 32 = BC and BD = 4 + 16 = 20 = AC. AB = BA. By SSS, △ ABC ≅ △ BAD.

29. HL looks like SSA, but it works. Use the Pythagorean theorem to explain why.

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse c and one leg a give the other leg b = c2 − a2. So all three sides match, and SSS applies.
