6.1Special Right Triangles

  • A 45-45-90 triangle is half of a square. The legs are equal. If a leg is s, the hypotenuse is s2.
  • A 30-60-90 triangle is half of an equilateral triangle. The short leg s is across from 30°. The long leg is s3. The hypotenuse is 2s.
  • To go from the hypotenuse to a leg of a 45-45-90 triangle, divide by 2 and rationalize: 102 = 52.
  • The marks on a figure show which pattern to use. A picture alone does not.
Example: a 45-45-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 10. Each leg is 52 ≈ 7.1. A 30-60-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 14. The short leg is 7 and the long leg is 73 ≈ 12.1.

6.2The Trigonometric Ratios

  • Name the sides from the angle you are using: the opposite side is across from it, the adjacent side is next to it, and the hypotenuse is across from the right angle.
  • sin A = oppositehypotenuse, cos A = adjacenthypotenuse, tan A = oppositeadjacent. Say SOH CAH TOA.
  • The ratios depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle. Similar triangles (AA) have the same ratios.
  • tan A is the slope of a line that makes angle A with the x-axis.
  • Set the calculator to degree mode.
Example: a right triangle has legs 5 and 12 and hypotenuse 13. For the angle across from 5: sin A = 513 ≈ 0.385, cos A = 1213 ≈ 0.923, tan A = 512 ≈ 0.417.

6.3Solving for a Side

  • Pick the ratio that has the side you know and the side you want.
  • If the unknown x is on top, multiply: sin 40° = x12 gives x = 12 sin 40°.
  • If the unknown is on the bottom, divide: cos 35° = 20x gives x = 20cos 35°.
  • Do not round until the last step. Check that the hypotenuse is the longest side.
Example: x = 12 sin 40° ≈ 7.7. And x = 20cos 35° ≈ 24.4.

6.4Solving for an Angle

  • Use the inverse ratio when you know two sides and want the angle: sin−1, cos−1, or tan−1.
  • sin A = 713 means A = sin−1(713). Find the ratio first, then use the inverse key.
  • The two acute angles add to 90°. Subtract to find the other one.
  • A ramp of 1 : 12 has an angle of tan−1(112) ≈ 4.8°.
Example: A = sin−1(713) ≈ 32.6°. The other angle is 90° − 32.6° = 57.4°.

6.5Sine and Cosine of Complementary Angles

  • Two angles are complementary if they add to 90°. In a right triangle, the two acute angles are complementary.
  • sin A = cos(90° − A) and cos A = sin(90° − A).
  • tan A = sin Acos A and tan A · tan(90° − A) = 1.
  • Equal ratios do not mean equal angles. sin 40° and cos 40° are different.
Example: sin 25° ≈ 0.423 and cos 65° ≈ 0.423. They are equal. tan 25° · tan 65° ≈ 1.0.

6.6Angles of Elevation and Depression

  • Both angles are measured from the horizontal. Elevation is up. Depression is down.
  • The angle of elevation from A to B equals the angle of depression from B to A (alternate interior angles).
  • If the observer's eyes are above the ground, add the eye height to the height you find.
  • With two angles, write one equation for each right triangle and solve.
Example: a person stands 80 ft from a tower. Her eyes are 5.5 ft high. The angle of elevation to the top is 62°. Height: 80 tan 62° + 5.5 ≈ 156.0 ft.

6.7Modeling with Right Triangles

  • Six steps: sketch, label, choose a tool, solve, check, say the answer in a sentence with units.
  • Two sides and a missing side: Pythagorean theorem. An angle and a side: sine, cosine, or tangent. Two sides and a missing angle: inverse ratio.
  • A ramp rises 1 inch for every 12 inches of run. A roof pitch of 712 means tanθ = 712.
  • A bearing is measured clockwise from north. North distance = dcos(bearing). East distance = dsin(bearing).
  • On a map grid, the bearing is 90° minus the angle from east.
Example: a ship sails 40 nautical miles on a bearing of 060°. North: 40 cos 60° = 20. East: 40 sin 60° ≈ 34.6.
